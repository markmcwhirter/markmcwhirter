Culture, Study, Exegesis September 21, 2026

The Word and Modern Culture Posting

Modern U.S. culture has more influence than ever in our everyday lives. Views of morality and acceptable behavior have shifted significantly over the past few decades. Although there are variations of views of morality, overall it now deviates more and more from biblical principles. Within Christianity, there are significant differences on many key issues.

Today I am raising awareness to the differences between what the Word of God states about godly behavior and what many modern Christians now believe and are being taught. This difference has no basis in correct biblical study or analysis, but instead is in response to secular culture's pressure and influence on the individual and the church.

Modern society can pressure Christians to compromise biblical teaching by changing how they understand right and wrong. From a traditional Christian perspective, moral truth comes from God rather than personal preference or popular opinion. Jesus taught that moral behavior should be based on loving God and loving one’s neighbor (Matt. 22.37–40). However, modern American culture often emphasizes personal freedom, self-expression, and the right to choose one’s own beliefs. These values are not always wrong, but they can conflict with biblical teaching when individual desires become more important than obedience to God.

Entertainment, advertising, and social media strongly influence moral beliefs. When certain behaviors are repeatedly presented as normal or desirable, people may gradually become less likely to question them. Christians may then reinterpret, ignore, or remain silent about difficult biblical teachings because they fear criticism or rejection. This can affect beliefs about sexuality, marriage, money, honesty, forgiveness, and respect for human life. A recent survey found that Americans disagree greatly about many moral issues, showing that there is no single moral standard accepted throughout society.

Consumer culture can also weaken biblical values. Modern society often connects happiness with wealth, possessions, popularity, and achievement. The Bible, however, warns against greed and teaches that people cannot serve both God and money (Matt. 6.19–24). It calls believers to practice generosity, contentment, and concern for people in need (1 Tim. 6.6–10, 17–19). When churches focus mainly on success, comfort, attendance, or public influence, they may neglect teachings about sacrifice, humility, and service.

Political and social divisions create another danger. Christians may become more loyal to a political party, social movement, or national identity than to biblical principles. This can lead them to excuse dishonesty, cruelty, or injustice when those actions benefit their own group. Jesus taught that leadership should involve service rather than the pursuit of power (Mark 10.42–45). He also commanded his followers to love their enemies, while Paul instructed Christians not to seek revenge (Matt. 5.44; Rom. 12.17–21). These teachings challenge a culture that often rewards anger, insults, and public humiliation.

Cultural influence is not always negative. Modern society has sometimes pushed churches to examine whether prejudice, discrimination, or unfair traditions truly reflect biblical teaching. Cultural criticism can expose the difference between God’s commands and customs that Christians have mistakenly treated as biblical. Believers therefore should not automatically accept or reject every cultural change. They should carefully test ideas through Scripture, prayer, reason, and responsible interpretation. Paul’s instruction is to avoid being shaped by the world and instead develop a renewed mind capable of recognizing what is good (Rom. 12.2).

Ultimately, compromise often happens slowly. It may begin when people place comfort, approval, or personal desire above faithfulness to God. Christians can respond by studying Scripture carefully, listening to wise believers, admitting their own biases, and practicing their beliefs consistently. The goal is not to reject society completely, but to engage with it without allowing popular opinion to replace biblical conviction.
